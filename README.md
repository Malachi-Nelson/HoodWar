# HoodWar — Port Calder Open World

An open-world crew game that runs in the browser. One lake-port city and its airport, no rounds and no clock: take any car, helicopter or plane, take neighbourhoods for your crew and live off what they pay, rob the bank with your friends, run a drug lab, buy up businesses on your turf, call your homies, rank up, and stay ahead of the police and the other crews. Play alone or online with friends; bots are everyone else. Your cash, guns, rank, businesses, car, aircraft, lab and turf are saved as you play.

## The city

- **Six crews, ten neighbourhoods.** Bloods (red, west side), Crips (blue, dock side), Sex Money Murder (green, north side), Vice Lords (yellow, south side), Gangster Disciple Killers (white, south-west) and Gangster Disciples (black, south-east) each have a yard, cars in their colour and one neighbourhood to start with. Every street in the city belongs to one of the ten neighbourhoods (A to J along the top of the screen); the mall, the station, the market and the park in the middle of town belong to nobody at the start, and pay the most.
- **Holding turf.** Each neighbourhood has a hold meter. Hurting the crew that owns it wears its hold down: shooting its people, robbing its shops, wrecking its cars, taking out its lieutenants. The weaker the hold, the faster you take the neighbourhood by standing in its ring. A neighbourhood you have just taken starts at half hold and firms up while things are quiet. Every in-game hour (100 seconds) each of your crew's neighbourhoods pays out, and pays more the firmer your hold. Rival crews keep their own street crews on their corners, raid other crews' turf and start hood wars on yours.
- **Notoriety.** Hurt a crew and it marks you, one to five skulls beside your cash. From two skulls it sends cars of hitters after you. Stay out of its sight and it lets it go; dying clears it, along with police heat.
- **Headshots.** A player's headshot kills anyone in one shot.
- **Jobs around town.** Two or three at a time, listed on screen with distance and time left: an armoured truck to shoot to a stop and empty, a hit on a rival lieutenant, a hood war to defend, a car to steal for the chop shop, a stash to carry back to your yard, a street race, and deals going down between two crews (crash one and the cash is yours).
- **Guns.** Pistol, Micro SMG, Pump Shotgun, Assault Rifle, Marksman Rifle, Dual Glocks on drum magazines (a Glock switch makes them full auto), the M60, a grenade launcher and a bazooka, plus grenades that hit hard. Better guns open as you rank up.
- **Cars.** Faster on the whole, with a muscle car and a supercar on the streets. The car shop adds armor, engine tunes, nitrous (Shift), better grip, bulletproof glass, neon and paint; the phone sells cars outright.
- **People with somewhere to be.** Pedestrians walk between places and stop there: benches, shop windows, food carts, market stalls, ball courts, bus stops, a speaker someone has left playing. Vendors and clerks keep their stalls and counters, workers carry crates across the docks and the foundry yard, and officers walk a beat. Gunfire scatters them; a gun pointed at a clerk gets hands in the air.
- **The rest of it.** Day and night, fewer people about late at night, pigeons that take off when you run through them, gulls over the lake, steam from manholes, smoke from the foundry stack, ships and boats on the water, an airliner coming and going at the airport, the ball going up on the courts, short lines from the people you pass, crowd noise, birds by day and crickets at night.
- **Weather.** Clear spells, cloud, rain, thunderstorms and fog off the lake come and go every few minutes, the same for everyone in a session. In the rain the streets go dark and wet and cars slide more, fewer people are out, some put up umbrellas and the rest hurry; a storm brings lightning and thunder; fog closes the city in. The host can set it from the dev panel.
- **Buses.** Two Calder Transit buses run a loop round the middle of town, clockwise, stopping at six shelters. People waiting get on and others get off. Walk up to one standing at a stop and press F to ride for $5; the next stop shows by the speedometer, and F gets you off. A bus whose driver is gone is just a big car to steal, and the depot sends another.
- **On the street.** Buy something from a food cart that has someone working it to get some health back. In the afternoon and at night locals shoot dice on a few corners: walk into the circle and bet $50 to $1,000, high roll wins and a tie is a push. Win too much too fast and they decide your dice are loaded, scatter, and send a carload after you; pull a gun or fire one and the pot is left on the ground. Now and then a purse snatcher grabs a bag near you and runs: bring him down, then hand the purse back for a reward or keep it (take it home like any cash). Sometimes a crew member is already holding up one of the corner stores: take them down before they get away with the till and the owner pays you. Some people are out walking their dogs.

**Play:** https://malachi-nelson.github.io/HoodWar/

Plays with a keyboard and mouse, a controller, or on a phone or tablet held sideways. The controls are listed on the menu.

## Controls

**Controls** on the menu (or the pause screen) lists every action on foot, in a car and flying, with two keys or buttons each. Tap a box and press the key, mouse button (side buttons too) or wheel you want; Backspace clears a box. Where two actions share a key the page says so, and the one made for the situation wins (Q is the radio in a car and the next gun on foot). Esc, `, 1–0, - and = stay as they are. **ADS** (aim down sights) can be held or toggled, and the mouse speed is on the same page.

A **controller** works as soon as it is connected (Xbox, PlayStation and most others): the left stick moves, drives and flies, the right stick looks, the triggers aim and fire on foot and drive in a car, the d-pad and A/B work the phone and the shops, and Menu pauses. Its buttons have their own tab on the Controls page, with look speed, inverted look and aim help. Bindings are kept in the browser.

## Heists

- **Calder Savings Score (co-op).** Phone › Heists › Calder Savings Score. Three setups first, any of them done by anyone in the session, kept for the session:
  - **Getaway car**: a fast car sits on a rival crew's corner with three of them watching it. Take it and park it at the getaway spot behind the bank (a GETAWAY sign hangs over it from then on).
  - **Drill and charges**: a crate of gear with four of a crew on it. Carry it to the getaway car.
  - **Case the bank**: stand at the three marked spots in and around the bank like a customer, guns away and no stars.
- **Then in loud or quiet** (from rank 4). **Loud** needs the gear: the alarm goes as you walk in, guards and the police come, someone has to hold the vault while the drill runs, and when it jams someone has to start it again. **Quiet** needs the bank cased: walk in with your guns away, slip behind the counter while the guards are looking the other way, and hack the time lock on the back-office wall (stand at the panel for a few seconds); twenty seconds later the vault opens by itself. A guard who sees a gun out, someone behind the counter or at the vault, or a bag on someone's back grows suspicious (the job line shows how much); a guard hurt or a shot fired and the alarm goes. Quiet bags are worth 40% more, and the bank's silent alarm only calls the police 45 seconds after the vault opens.
- **The money comes out in bags**: one on each back, slow going (no sprinting), more bags the more of you there are. A bag counts once it is in the getaway car, and the car has to reach the drop, across town with no stars on you. Anyone who goes down drops their bag; if the getaway car burns, so does everything loaded in it. The take is split between everyone in the session.
- **Cargo Score** (rank 6, $4,000): the airport's cargo yard. Get near and it is restricted ground: three stars and the airport police. Crack the lock on the end container of the west row (stand at it ten seconds), then carry the gold ($6,000 a bag) to any car or helicopter one of you is driving: that is the getaway now. Fly it out if you like.
- **Jewelry store.** Gold Rush Jewelers in the mall, from rank 2: smash the five cases and get away.

## Playing together

- **Down, not out.** Online, when your health runs out while someone on your side is still up, you go down instead of dying: you bleed out for 30 seconds while the bots leave you alone. A teammate stands over you and presses USE ("Pick up …") for a couple of seconds and you are back with 40% health. Go down again within 25 seconds and it is for real. USE while down gives up.
- **Pings.** Z or the middle mouse button (PING on a touch screen, the right stick click on a controller) marks whatever is under your crosshair for your side: a spot, an enemy, a car or the police, with your name on it, on the radar too. Playing alone, a ping sends your homies there.
- **Your side down the left of the screen**: each teammate's health, how far off they are, whether they are down, in a car or carrying a bag.
- **Coming back near the others.** Die online and you come back on foot a short way from the nearest teammate who is up, not across town.
## The drug business

- **The lab.** Buy a unit in the industrial district for $25,000 (Phone › Drug business, or walk up to the door). It makes a unit of product every in-game hour, up to 20, and keeps making it (more slowly) while you are away. Walk up to your lab's door to open its books.
- **Supplies.** Every unit uses 5% of the lab's supplies; at nothing, it stops. **Buy supplies** ($3,000 for +50%) or **Steal supplies**: a supply van sits on a crew's corner with three of them watching it; drive it to the lab for +60%.
- **Upgrades.** Better equipment (60% faster, $15,000), a better cook ($800 a unit instead of $600, $12,000), more storage (40 units, $9,000), security (raids come half as often and two of your crew defend the lab, $12,000).
- **Dealers.** Put up to three dealers on corners your side holds ($2,500 each): each sells a unit every couple of in-game hours at 85% while your side keeps the neighbourhood. Now and then one gets picked up by the police.
- **Raids.** Once there are eight units or more in the lab, a crew comes for them every so often: get there and drop all five before they finish, or lose half.
- **Selling.** Smuggle the lot in a van across town (a crew tails it), sell wholesale, deal it on the corners, fence it, or sell it to another player.

## Deals and briefcases

Deals go the Sub Rosa way: what changes hands is a briefcase someone has to carry. A case of cash, of product or a courier's package is in your hand until you put it down (USE when nothing else is in reach) or go down, and whoever picks it up owns it. Cash is yours once it is home: your side's yard or your lab. Product goes into your lab, or to the fence at Calder Auto Body for $450 a unit.

- **Wholesale** (Phone › Deals, five units or more): a crew pays 30% over the street price at a meet. You carry the case of product to them yourself. One time in four they came with guns instead of money; drop them and the cash case their buyer was showing is yours, and you still have the product.
- **Courier job**: carry a package across town to a buyer ($2,200 to $4,200). Word sometimes gets out.
- **Between players**: Phone › Deals › Offer product to (another player): pick a size and price. They answer from their phone; if they take it, the seller gets a case of product, the buyer a case of their own cash, and both get the meet on the map. Then it is up to the two of you: swap cases, or don't.
- **Texts**: Phone › Deals › Text (a player): meet me here, need backup, got the goods, bring the money, deal is off, watch your back, with where you are marked on their map.

## The airport, helicopters and planes

- **Port Calder International** sits on an island across the harbour, over the Harbor Bridge from the docks: a runway with airliners landing and taking off, a terminal with four gates, hangars, a cargo yard and helipads. Nobody's crew runs it.
- **Helicopters.** A Skylark (four seats, no guns) waits on the airport's pads for anyone to take. The airport police keep a **Hornet** gunship on their pad, with nose guns and rockets; their guards are on it, and taking it brings three stars. W A S D fly it, Space and Shift (UP and DOWN on a touch screen) climb and descend, the mouse turns it, click fires the guns and right-click or G the rockets. Homies get in with you and shoot from the doors.
- **Planes.** Two **Mallards** (a forgiving two-seat prop) and a **Starling** (a four-seat business jet) stand on the apron for anyone to take. Taxi onto the runway (or any long straight), throttle up with Space (UP on a touch screen, RT on a controller), and once it is quick enough pull back with S (the stick back) to lift the nose. In the air it flies toward where you look with the mouse: point the camera and it banks and climbs to follow. W and S pitch and A and D bank it directly, which is how the stick flies it on a touch screen or a controller. Shift (DOWN, LT) throttles back. Below its stall speed the wings stop holding it up and the nose drops: the HUD shows the throttle and warns you. To land, come in slow and shallow with the wings level and settle onto the runway, then throttle right back and hold Shift to brake. A hard or crooked touchdown, a dive into the ground or anything in the way wrecks it. The Mallard lifts off after about 30 m; the Starling wants over 120 m and tops out near 165 mph. Friends ride along in the other seats.
- **Your own.** Phone › Aircraft sells a Skylark ($45,000, rank 4), a Hornet ($95,000, rank 8), a Mallard ($60,000, rank 5) or a Starling ($160,000, rank 9), kept on file. A helicopter is set down on the nearest helipad whenever you call (the airport's, the strip mall car park or the docks); a plane waits in one of the two boxed stands on the airport's south apron, beside the end of the runway.
- **Getting out.** Jump out of anything up high and a parachute opens. Ditch in the water and you swim ashore.
- **Police helicopter.** At three stars a police helicopter joins the chase: it circles where you were last seen with two marksmen in the doors and, after dark, a searchlight on you. It only knows what it can see, so get under cover to lose it, or shoot it down.

## Playing the police

Pick **Police** under the crews on the menu. Officers start outside the El station (or at the airport police hangar), get an SMG and armor from the armory every time they come back (a rifle too from rank 4), and are never wanted. The crews shoot at an officer on sight. Pay: a wage every in-game hour, the going rate for every gangster you take down, and $300 a star for a wanted player. Killing a bystander costs $250. The phone calls **Backup** (officers who walk with you) and a **Cruiser** with lights and siren (H); the airport police's gunship is yours to take. Dispatch tells you on the radio when another player becomes wanted, and wanted players show on your radar.

## Rank, phone, businesses and your car

- **Rank.** Kills (more for a headshot), taking neighbourhoods, jobs, robberies, buying businesses and upgrading cars all earn RP, shown in the blue badge and bar under your cash. Ranking up opens guns in the gun shop (Pump Shotgun at rank 2, Dual Glocks at 3, Assault Rifle at 4, the Glock switch at 5, Marksman Rifle at 6, grenade launcher at 7, M60 at 8, bazooka at 10), more homies, the fixer, and +5 health every other rank.
- **The phone** (T, or PHONE on a touch screen). **Homies**: two to four of your crew come round the corner, walk with you, get in your car and shoot at whoever is shooting at you (from rank 2). **Mechanic**: brings your car to a kerb near you. **Fixer**: the police forget you, $200 a star (from rank 3). **Work**: lines up a job straight away. **Heists**, **Drug business**, **Online · cars** and **Aircraft** are on it too.
- **Businesses.** A shop or two in every neighbourhood will sell to the crew that runs it; the ones for sale have a green ring at the door. Each pays its owner every in-game hour while your crew holds the neighbourhood, and nothing while somebody else does.
- **Car shop.** Stop in your crew's garage, or the car shop on Gas & Auto Row: repairs, resprays (which also lose the police, if they cannot see you go in), armor plating, an engine tune, and **Make it yours**, which keeps the car on file. Your car waits at a kerb near you when you start, and the mechanic brings it, upgrades and all.

## Touch screens

On a touch screen the game shows on-screen controls: a stick for the left thumb (push it all the way to sprint), drag anywhere on the right to look, and buttons that change between on foot, in a car and flying a helicopter; PHONE opens the phone. While you fire or aim, aim help eases the view toward a hostile near the crosshair. **Move buttons** (on the menu or the pause screen) lets you drag any button somewhere else and change its size and see-through level, separately for on foot, in a car and flying. Move buttons also changes what any button does (pick an action for it), hides the ones you never use, and adds up to eight buttons of your own for whatever you like. **ADS** is a button of its own: tap it on and off, or set it to work while held. Look speed, aim help and a fixed or floating stick are on the same row. The layout is kept in the browser.

## Saving

Cash, guns, grenades, armour, your rank, your businesses, your car, your helicopter or plane, your lab and its stock, your crew and your crew's turf are saved in the browser as you play. Your turf and businesses keep paying while you are away: an hour of income for every real hour, for up to eight hours.

**Copy save code** on the menu gives you a code to paste into **Load save code** on another phone or computer. On the Claude page the save is also kept with your Claude account, so it is there wherever you open the page signed in.

## Online co-op

1. Everyone opens the page. The line under the lobby buttons should turn green and say "Online · public room".
2. Type a name. One player clicks **Open lobby**; everyone else clicks **Join lobby**, then **Ready up**.
3. The lobby leader clicks **Start session**. Anyone who turns up later clicks **Join session** and drops in where it stands.

Everyone on the plain link shares one public room. **Make a private room** gives you a link with a code for just your group.

The leader picks **Players** before starting: **One crew** puts everyone in the leader's crew (or on the force, if the leader picked the police), and players cannot hurt each other. **Rival sides** lets everyone pick their own crew or the police: then each side takes turf for itself, each player is paid for their own side's neighbourhoods, and anyone on another side is fair game. The leader's browser runs the city (bots, traffic, turf, jobs, police), so the session starts from the leader's saved turf. When the leader leaves, the session carries on: the next player takes over the city where it stands (turf, time of day and everyone's cars stay; people and traffic start fresh around you), and anyone who joins later comes to them. A player who loses their own connection keeps playing (the line under the clock says Reconnecting) and drops back in when it returns; a host who comes back after others took over joins their city. Each player keeps their own cash, guns, rank, businesses and car; turf income, job rewards and the RP for them are paid to everyone in the session, and homies follow whoever called them.

Each player's browser posts its state to two free public MQTT relays at once, which pass it to the others in the room; if one relay fails or is blocked on someone's network, play carries on through the other. There is no game server and nothing connects browser to browser. The line under the clock shows who runs the city, how many are in it and the relay round trip. If someone in the room is on a newer version of the page, the lobby says so and offers to reload.

## What is in the repository

| Path | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `index.html` | The whole game, built into one page. This is what GitHub Pages serves. |
| `src/` | The game in parts, joined in number order by `./build.sh`. Edit these, not `index.html`. |
| `relay/relay.html` | Online play for the public page: the relay connection and the room link under the lobby. |
| `mqtt.min.js` | MQTT.js 4.3.7 (MIT licence), used by the relay. |
| `build.sh`, `check.sh` | Build the page; build and syntax-check it. |
| `test/` | Headless browser checks. They need Playwright, Chrome and `npm install`. |

three.js r128 (MIT licence) is loaded from cdnjs.

`./build.sh` also writes `dist/claude.html`, the same game without the relay, for publishing as a Claude artifact, where multiplayer uses Claude's own shared room instead.

If a change alters what players send each other, build with a new `PROTO` (the default is `v4`; for example `PROTO=v5 ./build.sh`) and change the room name in `NET.connect` (`src/110-net.js`), so that old tabs and new ones do not end up in the same room.
